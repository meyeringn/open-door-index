Can every Philadelphian get in the room?

Public meetings are where the city decides things. This index checks whether City Council and ten city boards tell Disabled residents how to take part, using only what each body publishes itself. Every finding is sourced and dated.

    A door opens wider for every one of nine access practices a body documents. Partial credit opens it half as far.

    Your rights at a public meeting

    You can see the agenda a day ahead

    Pennsylvania's Sunshine Act, as amended by Act 65 of 2021, requires agencies with a website to post meeting agendas there at least 24 hours before the meeting, and also at the meeting site and their main office. In October 2026, the legislature passed SB 1150 to close a loophole that let boards add items at the last minute by majority vote; Spotlight PA reports Governor Shapiro signed it.

    You can ask for an accommodation

    Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, City programs, including public meetings, must communicate as effectively with Disabled people as with everyone else. The City says it provides reasonable modifications and communication aids at no cost. Requests go to the ADA Compliance Director at ADA.Request@phila.gov.

    How to ask

    Ask early, in writing, and name what you need: an ASL interpreter, CART live captions, large print, a step-free route, or a way to comment remotely. Copy the board's own contact if it publishes one.

    If the answer is no

    Ask for the reason in writing, then contact the City's Office for People with Disabilities. You can also send a tip to this index, so the next person finds out before they go.

    The Open Door standard

    Every practice in this index is cheap to fix with one paragraph. Any board can paste this onto its agendas and meeting pages, fill in the brackets, and move up the index the same day.

    Access paragraph for agendas

    Everyone is welcome at this meeting. It is held at [address], which has [a step-free entrance at ___ / an accessible restroom on floor ___]. You can also join and speak live by video at [link] or by phone at [number].

    Live captions [are provided / can be requested]. To request an ASL interpreter, CART captioning, materials in large print or another format, or any other accommodation, contact [name] at [email] or [phone] by [day and time]. Requests after that time will still be met whenever possible.

    You can send written comments to [email] until [deadline]. A recording of this meeting will be posted at [link] within [number] days.

    How the index works

    Open Door Index looks only at what each body publishes about its own meetings: its meeting page on phila.gov or its own site, and the instructions on its most recent agenda. That is what a resident sees before deciding whether to go. A body may do more in practice than it writes down; when it does, publishing it is the fix, and we will update the score as soon as we can see it.

    Each body gets a count of practices met out of nine. Partial findings are shown but not counted. There are no letter grades, because the point is a to-do list, not a ranking.

    Where a phila.gov document's web address includes its upload time, we use that time to check whether an agenda went up at least 24 hours ahead. We say so in the finding.

    The SEPTA Board is regional rather than a City body and is not included. The index started with eleven bodies and will grow.

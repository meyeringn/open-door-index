# Open Door Index

**Can every Philadelphian get in the room?** A nonpartisan, evidence-based scorecard of whether Disabled Philadelphians can take part in public meetings of City Council and ten city boards and commissions.

🌐 **Live index:** https://meyeringn.github.io/open-door-index/

**Data last verified:** November 2026

---

## Why it exists

Public meetings are where Philadelphia decides things. If a Deaf resident can't get an interpreter, a wheelchair user can't find out whether the room is step-free, or someone who can't leave home can't speak remotely, they're shut out of those decisions. Most of these barriers are cheap to fix, and the first step is simply writing down how to take part.

## What it checks

Each body is checked against nine access practices, using only what it publishes about its own meetings:

1. Agenda posted at least 24 hours ahead
2. Remote live participation, by video or phone
3. Written comment accepted
4. Live captions
5. ASL interpretation on request
6. An accommodation contact published for meetings
7. Venue accessibility described
8. Recordings archived for the public
9. Agenda in readable text

Each finding is marked **Met**, **Partial**, **Not found**, or **Not yet audited**, with the source and the date it was checked. Scores are counts ("5 of 9"), not grades.

## What we found (November 2026)

- No body documents live captioning.
- One body, City Council, documents how to get an ASL interpreter.
- No body describes whether its meeting room is accessible.
- Five bodies let residents speak live from home.

## Bodies covered

City Council, Board of Education, City Planning Commission, Zoning Board of Adjustment, Board of Ethics, City Commissioners, Historical Commission, Art Commission, Civil Service Commission, Board of License & Inspection Review, and the Mayor's Commission on People with Disabilities. The SEPTA Board is regional and not included.

## Also inside

- **Your rights at a public meeting:** the Sunshine Act's 24-hour agenda rule (including October 2026's SB 1150) and how to request an accommodation
- **The Open Door standard:** a fill-in-the-blank access paragraph any board can paste onto its agendas
- **A field checklist** for Philadelphia Documenters, journalists, and residents who attend meetings
- **Printable report cards** for each body, and the full dataset as CSV

## Disclosure

Built by Nico Meyering, who chairs the Mayor's Commission on People with Disabilities. That Commission was scored first, with the same rules as everyone else. This is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Committee of Seventy, the City of Philadelphia, or any body listed.

## Corrections

Every correction is checked and dated before it changes a score. Use the tip form on the site, [open an issue](https://github.com/meyeringn/open-door-index/issues), or email meyeringn@gmail.com.

## Tech and accessibility

One self-contained `index.html` with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No account, no tracking of anything you type; page visits are counted anonymously with GoatCounter. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA; see the [accessibility statement](ACCESSIBILITY.md).
